Introduction to Cultural Studies

When I did my AMI training I created Albums of presentations, and exclusively used these albums for many years. I use them still, but no longer exclusively; over the course of almost 20 years I have accumulated a collection of alternative albums containing alternative lists of presentations and alternative ways of giving those presentations. I believe that there are many flaws and omissions in the AMI Albums and feel it is important to utilise all resources in order to improve my class, so I’ve been giving non-AMI sanctioned presentations for many years now. In this section I’ll detail some of the options that are available and why I chose some but not others, in order to illustrate the important point that there is a whole world of information, knowledge and experience out there, and some of it is quite educational.

As part of a rich exploration of the different cultures of the world, children are introduced to the knowledge, ideas, beliefs, customs, and social behaviour of each continent. Using materials such as globes, puzzle maps, science experiment kits, and cultural artifacts, children learn about diverse cultures, developing their curiosity, global awareness, and appreciation for diversity. The cultural materials are often categorised into the following units of study:

Biology

In a Montessori environment, children explore three of the seven branches of biology:

1. Botany2. Zoology3. Earth Sciences.

Botany

This area of the curriculum provides as an introduction to plant anatomy and plant life, helping children to identify leaf shapes and learn about life-cycles. Materials include The Botany Cabinet, Botany Puzzles, Botany Cards (cards depicting plants, and their classifications), Parts of the Plant and Plant Life-Cycle materials, as well as Care of Plants and Gardening. A simple concrete introduction to plants and their care is having the children water the plants, often with the guidance of an older child.

Planting new plants or transplanting ones that have outgrown their pot is also a wonderfully tactile exercise for individual children or small groups.

Children are also showed how to care of plants by cleaning or pruning their leaves, which serves as a practical way to begin caring for their environment while also serving as an introduction to plant structure.

Another way to encourage care of the environment is by having a ‘flower angel,’ where each week a different family buys flowers for the class, which their child prepares and places throughout the room.

Some schools I’ve worked at had access to a garden (or planter boxes), so we were able to take Montessori outside.

The Botany Puzzles serve as an introduction the study of parts of a plant / flower / tree / etc., making the connection between concrete and abstract.

The Botany Cabinet is used to introduce children to the variety leaf shapes and how we categorise them; matching the shapes of leaves from the Botany Cabinet with real leaves also links the abstract and the concrete.

Zoology

Using materials including Puzzle Cards, Models, Classification Cards and study of assorted Life-Cycles, children are introduced to different animals, their anatomy, habits, and classification into one of five kingdoms: Fish, Amphibians, Reptiles, Birds and Mammals. Children first make the Zoology puzzles inside the frame and later increase the challenge by making a puzzle outside the frame, eventually making all of the puzzles outside the frame at the same time.

Many classes have a pet such as a fish, turtle, rabbit, etc, which the children care for, and I’ve introduced slugs and insects including silkworms and praying mantises into the class. There’s life all around us of course, and the children are both alert to it and curious about it: bugs, lizards, birds, worms … it’s all fascinating to them, and there’s few things they enjoy more than studying them up close.

Earth Science

Also known as Physical Geography, this is the study of the Earth's air, water and solid matter. Children are introduced to two of its aspects, Geology and Meteorology, using materials such as Land and Water Forms, the Land and Water Globe, Land, Air and Water cards, and Weather Charts.

Land and Water Forms

These are five sets of opposite forms:

Lake / Island

Gulf / Peninsula

Strait / Isthmus

Bay / Cape

Lake System / Archipelago

The children choose a set and then pour water into the moulds, and the guide provides the definitions:

“An island is a body of land surrounded by water. A lake is a body of water surrounded by land.”

After this concrete introduction, the children are then presented with a range of abstract cards, gradually increasing in complexity and detail.

Land and Water Form Three-Part CardsLand and Water Form Definitions

Land, Air and Water

The children learn to divide objects into the categories of land, air or water, first using objects (concrete) and later using pictures (abstract).

Meteorology

Children record the weather each day, a practical introduction to this topic.

Concrete to abstract: the children are next presented with three-part cards of different types of weather, clouds, etc.

Arts and Crafts

To follow the interest of a meteorologically-minded child, there are innumerable weather-related ideas available online.

Typhoon

The second most fun aspect of meteorology (#1 is snow, of course).

Human Geography / Political Geography

Human Geography focuses on aspects of the environment that are man-made (as distinct from Physical Geography, which deals with features of the environment that were created by nature) and focuses on human activity - how people interact with their environment and with each other. The topic introduces concepts that illustrate cultural diversity and help children learn about different continents, countries and cultures using materials such as Geography Folders, Flags and Puzzle Maps of the Planisphere and the continents: North America, South America, Europe, Asia, Africa, Oceania and Antarctica.

Children are introduced to the materials in this order:

  1. Sandpaper Globe - water is smooth and land is rough (sandpaper)
  2. Continents Globe: Oceans are blue and Continents are coloured
  3. Puzzle Map of the Continents
  4. Puzzle Map of the child’s own continent
  5. Puzzle Map of the child’s own country (when available)
  6. Puzzle Maps of the remaining continents

They first complete the puzzles inside the frame and then, to increase the challenge, outside the frame,

Later they will create their own maps by tracing the outline of the continents on thick paper, punch holes using pushpins, then glue them onto thick paper.

After this tactile introduction, the next materials are from seven continent and country kits, containing three-part cards of the plants, animals, culture (houses, food, landmarks/places, musical instruments) of each continent.

Flags

Children are also introduced to the flags of different countries, beginning with their own country and continent or, if they wish, the country where their school is located. Once the child can identify a flag, they can colour and make their own.

History

History in a Montessori classroom is the study of the passage of time and how we measure it: in seconds, minutes, hours, weeks, months, seasons and years, using materials that include Timelines, Events Charts and The Clock. I try to accumulate a large collection of sand-timers, as they’re one of the oldest and most practical ways to measure time.

The 12 months of the year are punctuated by many events which mark the passing of time: the first day of school in August, Autumn and Halloween, Winter and Christmas, Spring and Chinese New Year, St. Patrick’s Day, etc.

In China the 24 Solar Terms are a wonderful way to reflect upon the passing of time.

Arts and Crafts

There are a great deal of seasonal materials available in a wide array of topics related to the seasons.

The Clock

Also introduced to small groups or 3 or 4 children, in the following order of presentations:

  1. Placing the numbers
  2. The Short Hand = hours
  3. Skip counting by 5’s
  4. Half-hours, then quarter-hours
  5. The Long Hand = minutes

This presentation can only be given to a child who is older than 4 ½, as #3 makes clear.

Three-Part Cards for the hours, half-hours, quarter-past and quarter-to, which can also be used as a writing exercise.

Older children are introduced to more abstract concepts such as the past, present and future.

Science

Physical Science introduces concepts such as energy, motion and magnetism, and is designed to spark curiosity, encourage hands-on exploration, and foster an understanding of scientific principles and the basics of categorization and opposites, for example sink or float, magnetic or non-magnetic, tracking the transit of Venus, and experiments such as Surface Tension.

When I worked in Beijing, an Elementary teacher was demonstrating the properties of dry ice to his class and was kind enough to allow us to come for a demonstration. He was also kind enough to invite us to join his class to observe the transit of Venus.

Another day a very curious little boy named Tony asked, “Why is it sometimes day and sometimes night?” The class didn’t have Globe of the Continents, so we took a sphere and a flashlight into a closet, and as another boy held the flashlight, Tony rotated the sphere and I explained that when one side of the sphere is bright the other is dark. The following week -after we’d bought the Globe of the Continents- I showed him where China was located on the globe and he rotated it, so it faced away from the sunshine streaming through the window, and I explained in this way why it’s sometimes day and sometimes night.

A Pair of Experiments

Experiment to Demonstrate Capillary Action

Procedure

1. Prepare flowers or a cabbage leaf:

If needed, trim the stems at an angle under water to help them absorb the coloured water.

2. Prepare the coloured water:

Fill the glasses or vases with water and add a few drops of different food colouring to each.

3. Place the flowers in the coloured water:

Put one flower into each glass or vase, making sure the cut end of the stem is submerged.

4. Observe the colour change:

Over time, you will see the flower petals start to change colour as the coloured water

travels up the stem through capillary action.

Explanation

Capillary action

This is the process where liquids move up narrow spaces without the need for external forces like gravity.

How it works in flowers

Water molecules are attracted to each other (cohesion) and to the walls of the xylem (the tubes in the stem) (adhesion).

This attraction, combined with evaporation from the leaves (transpiration), pulls water up through the stem.

Observation

As the water travels up the stem, it carries the dissolved food colouring with it, resulting in the flower petals changing colour.

Candle in Limited Air Experiment

Combustion

A lit candle is placed in a shallow dish of water, and a glass or jar is inverted over it. As the candle burns, it consumes the oxygen inside the inverted container.

Oxygen Depletion

When the oxygen supply is significantly reduced, the candle flame extinguishes because combustion requires oxygen.

Pressure Change

As the oxygen is consumed and the candle extinguishes, the air inside the container cools, leading to a decrease in air pressure within the container.

Water Rise:

The higher atmospheric pressure outside the container then pushes the water from the dish up into the inverted glass or jar to equalize the pressure difference.

Music and Movement

Children are innately musical, and love singing and chanting and moving and dancing, and there are many ways to stoke this interest, including providing a selection of instruments for them to play, listening to music using headphones, having a trained musician play instruments or give a music class, singing and dancing together at circle time, or having an outing to a classical music concert or having musicians perform in the class.

Music - Playing Instruments

Activities such as singing, dancing, playing musical instruments, and rhythm games develop auditory skills, rhythm, and coordination, using materials such an assortment of musical instruments such as bells, drums, etc. and three-part cards of famous composers, fostering skills including physical coordination, auditory discrimination, and a love for music.

Music - Headphones

They’re not part of the AMI Sensorial curriculum, but children love putting on headphones and listening to music or audiobooks, and I’ve found it to be a passive but effortless but nevertheless effective way to reinforce any song I want them to learn, for example when I’m rehearsing for the end of term or end of year performance, or if Christmas or any other significant holiday is approaching.
